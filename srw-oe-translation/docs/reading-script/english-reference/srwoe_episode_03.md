## Episode 3-1: Gundam Intervention

Shortly after war broke out between the Federation and Zeon, the Japanese archipelago was declared a special neutral zone, Kuril Islands included.

On one uninhabited island stood an observatory run directly by the Federation government. Zeon intelligence knew it was a secret Federation base.

Garma Zabi, commander of the Earth Attack Force, meant to raid it with a mobile cruiser, expose the treaty violation, and sway public opinion to Zeon.

North America and the North Pacific were still in Zeon hands, and the island lay well within range.

Sensing the attack, the Federation asked Japan's Defense Force, via its government, to guard it.

And so the Cyber Beast Force and Connect Force were dispatched to defend the observatory…

### The Labor investigation

Later investigation proved by experiment that the resonant voice of the Keronian Sgt. Keroro could cause the Labors to go rogue.

Analysis of the voice samples revealed that the HOS-equipped Labors' rampages were caused by a low frequency that only the Labors' sensitive sensors could detect.

The Labor incidents had been triggered when HOS-equipped Labors picked up low-frequency sound from resonance in tall, layered structures during strong winds.

This also showed that rewriting the HOS alone could not fully prevent rampages, and a new countermeasure program was to be written.

Sgt. Keroro, a potential trigger for rampages, was folded into Connect Force, with care taken to keep him away from Labor work zones for now.

## Editorial notes

- **Translation:** Dialogue and narration comes directly from the current project `pakiet_EN/00_dialogue.jsonl`, without rewriting or summarizing. The old example is not a translation source. In-game line wrapping is joined into normal paragraphs; meaningful narration paragraphs are retained, and parenthesized thoughts are italicized.

- **Names:** Customizable protagonist-name tokens use the project defaults **Seishiro Kusanagi**. English speaker names come from the character tables. Explicit anonymous or radio-channel cues remain anonymous or channel-based rather than inventing an identity. Inline engine cue prefixes are removed from the spoken text.

- **Shared given names:** Distinct characters who share a short name in this episode are distinguished by their full English names from the project tables.

- **Structure:** Mission titles come from the translated mission tables. Translated place/time entries are used as headings; other headings are editorial signposts, not additional game narration. There is no opening list of navigation links.

- **Conditional exchanges:** Blockquotes distinguish conditional or situational tutorial/battle exchanges and alternate resolutions. Not every such exchange is necessarily optional. Exact triggers are not asserted, and their source order is not presented as a guaranteed playthrough sequence.

- **DLC versions:** Identical passages in re-released versions of the same mission are represented once. Where a later version changes wording, its alternate passages are shown separately, with immediate context, under **DLC text variant**. These are not subsequent events in the story.

- **Coverage:** All **1,491 dialogue entries** and **57 narration entries** in this episode are accounted for. **1,021 entries** are displayed; **527 identical DLC-copy entries** are represented by their matching passages. Standalone objectives, tutorial help panels, engine/camera labels, and empty spacers are omitted.

- **Source edition:** Japanese **1.08**, **NPJH50521**. Scene grouping comes from `06_scenes.jsonl`; mission titles and character labels come from `05_mission_text.jsonl` and `07_names_characters.jsonl`.
