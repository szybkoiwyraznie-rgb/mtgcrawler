## Episode 11-1: Operation Retake the Ark

In space, the war between the Federation and Zeon had entered a new phase with the fall of Solomon.

Meanwhile, in neutral Japan, yet another major crisis had come to the surface.

The giant offshore platform built in Tokyo Bay, known as the Ark, had suddenly been seized by a horde of rampaging Labors.

Concluding that the HOS was not to blame for the rampage, Connect offered the Japanese government the services of Connect Force.

But fearing the collapse of the Babylon Project, the government refused to let Connect Force in.

Instead, it took the passive route of sealing off the Ark and waiting to see what happened.

However, when Connect's survey detected a Mucus Gate reading inside the Ark, the government was finally forced to make a decision.

Connect Force moved at once to resolve the crisis, drawing up a plan to spare the Ark's facilities as much damage as possible, and began final prep.

## SV2 and a New Ally

Faced with repeated wars and internal strife, the space colony nations had long since come to keep a minimal military to maintain public order.

Barred by the Federation from powerful weapons, they turned for self-defense to military Labors, already common among security companies.

Electric-powered Labors, whose engines could never explode, were common among colony civilians, and their parts were easy to come by.

Some colonies bought military Labors in bulk, but as a result, many of those Labors ended up in the hands of radical extremists.

There were also recent warnings that Labors made by the Mechanization Empire might be lurking among those already deployed.

In light of this, Connect Force went into space to tackle the troubles around the colonies.

Division 2 in particular, with its Patlabors and expertise in Labor crime, was set to respond to all sorts of incidents inside the colonies.

## Operation Stop the Solar Ray

### The ceasefire and its limits

The Solar Ray attack was stopped before it began.

In informal talks held soon after between Sovereign Degwin and General Revil, a ceasefire was concluded between Zeon and the Federation.

Under the treaty, A Baoa Qu was to be taken over by the Federation and Zeon's military disbanded, but Supreme Commander Gihren Zabi objected.

He claimed Degwin had bypassed parliament out of pity for a people suffering the Federation's vile acts. He would yield the fortress without blood, but insisted Zeon's sovereignty must be upheld.

As public opinion largely backed Gihren, a short- term settlement was reached: Zeon kept its forces intact, and new negotiations would be held.

Even so, with A Baoa Qu in hand, the Federation could send a fleet into Zeon's homeland at any time, and the tide seemed beyond reversing.

Some in the Federation military argued for tearing up the treaty and taking Side 3, but its radicals had lost their clout since Jamitov's arrest.

Meanwhile, Gihren Zabi, a strategic genius, had already devised a plan to break the deadlock, and bought time with political maneuvering.

With the ceasefire, fighting became localized, and the main battlefield shifted to politics.
